After our [2025 trip to Duinrell in The Netherlands]({{ site.baseurl }}/series/netherlands-25), we fancied going somewhere very different for our main break in 2026. With its summer sunshine and steep hills, in many ways, you couldn't more different than Lisbon.

I'd been to Lisbon once before, back in November 2012 with friends on a stag do and was keen to return there with the family some day. While it wasn't your typical stag do, it was definitely a different experience going with friends in the middle of November.

Somewhat unwisely, we decided to go there in the middle of August. Usually, prior to our Netherlands trips, we anxiously check the weather forecast, as like in the UK, you can never guarantee the weather, even in summer. Going to Portugal while much of mainland Europe was engulfed forest fires, we were checking the forecast and anxiously hoping it wouldn't be too hot.

As with our previous city breaks, we opted to stay in Airbnb accomodation. As a family of four, this is much more suitable than a hotel for us, though we'd come to find out that like in many European tourist hotspots, Airbnbs aren't particularly popular with locals.

Flying in from Manchester, we arrived in Lisbon in the early afternoon. Unlike when we went to Paris and had to manage the transfer from Beauvais–Tillé Airport to the city centre, Lisbon's Humberto Delgado Airport is only just outside of the city and pretty easy to get to.

When I stayed in Lisbon in 2012, it made sense to just use the Metro to get from the airport to my hotel, particularly as I was travelling alone and the hotel was pretty central. I was staying at the LX Boutique Hotel, which is located very close to Cais do Sodré station.

During our 2026 trip, with four of us travelling with lots of luggage and our Airbnb not that easy to access by public transport, it made more sense to get a taxi. Researching beforehand, people said to use Bolt and Uber over the local taxis, as they're substantially cheaper and you're less likely to be overcharged. We opted for a Bolt taxi and instantly regretted it, leaving the airport and getting stuck in traffic queues.

Our Airbnb apartment was located on Costa do Castelo, which you might guess from the name is located very close to Lisbon's famous Castelo de São Jorge. Indeed, we were on the street that circles the castle, immediately outside of its grounds.

Lisbon is famously situated on seven hills (how many other cities have seven hills? It's a popular number) and Castelo de São Jorge is located on one of those hills, an ideal vantage point for a castle, looking down on the city. This meant that our apartment was also near the top of that said hill, on a street restricted to residents and local taxis.

Our Bolt taxi would be unable to access our street, but fortunately, the manager of our apartment was able to give us details on where to be dropped off, closest to the apartment. So it was just a short walk from the taxi to our apartment, dragging our luggage up a cobbled hill.

## Accomodation

Upon deciding on a trip to Lisbon, we'd originally booked an Airbnb apartment on the edge of the Bairro Alto district of the city. Known as something as a party district, we thought our was far enough away to not be too noisy. After digging into more of reviews of it, it turned out there was a local restaurant which effectively turned into a club in the evenings, making for noisy nights.

Deciding this was going to be an issue, we went about finding alternative accomodation, which is how we ended up at the apartment near Castelo de São Jorge. The apartment was located in the Alfama neighbourhood, the oldest in Lisbon, with a history dating back to Roman times.

Having survived the 1755 earthquake, unlike the nearby downtown Baixa area, Alfama still retains its Moorish layout, with many narrow, winding streets, steep hills and lots of steps. While some might consider it a little scruffy, it has lots of character.

The thing that reall sold us on the apartment was the amazing view we had. With a balcony looking out over the city, with an unrestricted view, it provided a fantastic way to get our bearings. We'd end up spending most evenings sat out there, glass in hand, watching the world go by in Lisbon. It was definitely a good decision.

The apartment itself was lovely. Located in a small old block of apartments over five floors, the apartment was modern, clean and spacious. The location at the top of a hill also helped to regulate the temperature, with an evening breeze that would pass through the building. Fortunate, as there wasn't any air conditioning and temperatures were well over 30°C.

Despite the climb up, the apartment made for a great base for our stay in Lisbon, and is easily one of our favourite Airbnbs we've stayed in.

## Getting about in Lisbon

Lisbon can be a fantastically walkable city. I say can because there is a caveat, as from an accessibility it's not always that great. In addition to the steep hils, pavements are often narrow and paved with small cobbled stones. Known as Calçada Portuguesa (Portuguese pavement), the pavements are an iconic part of Lisbon's identity, but can often be uneven and very slippy when wet.

It is a very compact city though, so if you're good with hills and uneven pavements, you'll love walking around Lisbon. To tackle many of the city's most challenging hills, there's various elevators and funiculars. From the famous Santa Justa Lift, which is more tourist attraction than infrastructure to the Ascensor da Glória, the location of the tragic crash in 2025, which resulted in 16 deaths.

As a result of the tragic crash, all funicular services in the city were suspended, pending investigation. At the time of our trip, they were still closed, with the exception of the Graça funicular, which was given the go-ahead to open in April 2026 following inspection. Having only opened in 2024 and technically an inclined elevator, rather than a funicular, the Graça funicular is very different to the older funiculars in the city.

In addition to the famous elevators and funiculars, there's other more workmanlike elevators to help you up the hills. Elevador Castelo are two elevators that take you from Rua dos Fanqueiros in Baixa to Castelo de São Jorge, with the second elevator conveniently onto our street. Unsurprisingly, we'd end making use of Elevador Castelo a fair amount during our stay, though actually we only found out about the lower elevator during our second week!

## Public transport

### Trams

If you find walking a bit too much or want to explore further, then Lisbon's public transport is pretty fantastic. Most famous are the city's trams, both the iconic old trams and sleeker new trams. The little old trams really are a symbol of the city, with them functioning more as a tourist attraction than public transport.

Being small and agile, the little trams are perfect for negotiating the steep, winding medieval streets in the older parts of the city like Alfama, Mouraria, and Chiado. The most famous is the tram 28, which is so popular that you can expect to queue for two hours to ride it at peak times, if you're that way inclined.

The larger modern trams only ride along flat routes, such as the E15, which goes along the riverfront to Belém. These are large, modern light-rail style trams, accomodating far more people, but are far to big to negotiate the narrow streets of the old town.

During our trip, we ended up using both the old and new trams. We took a ride on the tram 12, as an alternative to the busy tram 28 as it covers a similar route, but is much easier to board. We also took the E15 tram multiple times, to get to Belém, LX Factory and Alcântara, which ended up being very convenient. 

### Buses

While the trams are great, they do suffer from one major flaw. The trams are often sharing space with motor traffic, which leads to inevitable delays, particularly on the streets around Baixa, which are often at a standstill.

For where there isn't a tram route, Lisbon's network of bus routes provide a fantastic alternative, though as with the trams, their weakness is having to share space with other motor traffic.

### Metro and trains

Lisbon also an underground metro system, enabling travel outside of the centre to the wider Lisbon area and to the airport. Like with most metros, this is useful for travelling greater distances and are quicker than trams and buses, with capacity to carry more passengers.

During our stay, we only used the metro once, as we didn't have much need for it. So we did it as much for the experience as convenience in getting to where we needed to.

Lisbon has several train stations, providing both commuter rail and intercity services across Portugal. Rossio Station is probably the most famous, with its beautiful façade and location close to Rossio Square. It's also the terminus for the train to Sintra, a popular tourist destination.

While nowhere near as attractive, Cais do Sodré Station is where to go for suburban services to the resort town of Cascais, stopping at other resorts along the way. Cais do Sodré is something of a transport hub, with metro trains, ferries, trams and buses stopping there.

We ended up catching the train from Cais do Sodré Station to Carcavelos, on one of our excursions to the beach. The train provides a cheap and convenient way to access the beach from Lisbon, which we found useful, though not the most comfortable in the heat!

### Navegante Card

Once we figured out where to obtain and load them, we found the [Navegante Occasional Card](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/buy/viva-viagem-card/){:target="_blank"} a convenient way to pay for public transport during our stay. The Navegante Occasional Card costs €0.50 to purchase and can be topped up with credit to use on public transport.

The card can be used on trams, buses, metro, funicular, ferries and certain suburban rail serivces and is generally a convenient way to pay. We just used them for pay-as-you-go zapping, though you can also buy day passes. The only real issue was topping up, which has to be done at a machine and can be a faff with four cards.

### Cycling

Unusually for me, I wasn't planning on doing a huge amount of cycling during our trip. Research before going pointed to cycling not being particularly great around where we were staying Alfama, both due to the hills and the lack of cycling infracture. I did consider hiring an e-bike, but decided it was too much bother, given I didn't think I'd get to use it enough.

This meant my experiences cycling in Lisbon largely consisted of an e-bike tour we did on our second day, and some time spent on the somewhat flawed Gira bike share, which would end up quite a frustrating experience. In addition to Gira, there's dockless bikes and scooters available from the likes of Lime, though I didn't use them while we were there.

Having gone there with low expections, I was pleasantly surprised by some of the cycling infrastructure in Lisbon, partly in some of the more modern parts of the city during the bike tour, but also riding along the river Belém. Something I'll come back to in further posts.

## Food and drink

Lisbon and Portugal is renouned for some fabulous food, particularly its seafood (fresh, tinned and salted), pasties and cakes. We were really looking forward to seeing what we could try, and having been there before, there was definitely some places I wanted to return to. 

This included the famous purveyor of tinned fish, [Sol e Pesca](https://maps.app.goo.gl/6eEJCqV3DLaUcQLS7){:target="_blank"}. Located on the popular Pink Street and once visited by Anthony Bourdain, Sol e Pesca became our local during my 2012 trip, with our hotel being a stone's throw away. It was great to return, sample some of the tinned fish based plates and enjoy a few jugs of sangria.

No visit to Lisbon is complete without a visit to [Pastéis de Belém](https://pasteisdebelem.pt/en/){:target="_blank"}, originator of the city's famous egg custard tart Pastéis de nata. Again, this is somewhere I went during my 2012 trip and high on the list to return to. We did this twice, firstly for a takeaway and again to sit in the café for lunch. 

We sampled Pastéis de nata from many other establishments in the city, which while not being the original, were still very good. This included [Manteigaria](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dThhw2MeVaPcZ8HF7){:target="_blank"} and [Nat'elier](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3GW11YkYkhePnXWN8){:target="_blank"}, which were both good and a lot easier to get to in Lisbon, without travelling to Belém.

Beyond tinned fish and Pastéis de nata, we also tried bifanas, a classic Portuguese sandwich at [Bifanas do Afonso](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mYusos9Qb5XLyQsM9){:target="_blank"} and at [Pastelaria Sambinha](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wKy3G13JaFWqBVF17){:target="_blank"} on a day trip to Almada, with the latter being the better.

Closer to our aparment, we had some nice middle eastern food at [Habibi Hub Lisboa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/cKR7igJAvvJXN2Gz7){:target="_blank"} and lovely fish at [São Jorge](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jFk59RnZidxYyKY56){:target="_blank"}, which is located at the top of the Elevador Castelo. [Teatro da Garagem (teatro taborda)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/q9VLw2KU7hJ973AbA){:target="_blank"} has a café with a view, which was a short walk from our apartment. We stopped here for afternoon drinks, though reviews indicated the food wasn't particularly great.

For breakfast, we had pasties at [Pastelaria Lagares](https://maps.app.goo.gl/e6rA6NSyP7xjEM1c9){:target="_blank"} nearby and [Confeitaria Nacional](https://maps.app.goo.gl/WQyhdSCM2EsU8nKR6){:target="_blank"} in Baixa, which I also got takeaways from, while out for run. While definitely a tourist hotspot, we really enjoyed Breakfast Lovers in [Graça](https://maps.app.goo.gl/rNXr3sqB2oaQb9Ez5){:target="_blank"} and [Chiado](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HFgFMwwayWFhBnGa8){:target="_blank"}.

On two visits to [LX Factory](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1H8MTxUkZWwjBdp67){:target="_blank"}, we had drinks and charcutery at [Patio](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vsGLGhdTC7sq1Kew7){:target="_blank"} and very nice tacos at [La Ruda](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VUJysc96BHSrh6Fh6){:target="_blank"}. There were more places at LX Factory we'd have liked to have tried, but didn't get opportunity.

Some other places we visited included [The Folks Belém](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5hDakgfhB3A1yeJG7){:target="_blank"} for a very pleasant lunch, [Pizzico Pizzeria](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8bq46dTKKyXhZyhQA){:target="_blank"}, which was very busy the first time we tried to get in, so had to return the next evening. On a day trip to Costa de Caparica, we spent most of the day at [Nona onda](https://maps.app.goo.gl/tdRr6HXesoxT2LWa8){:target="_blank"}, where we enjoyed some nice food and plenty of drinks. 

We did pop into [Time Out Market](https://maps.app.goo.gl/oXTrrEvADG9F4mLt6){:target="_blank"}, which is another popular tourist destination. Though this was just drinks, as none of the food options were particularly inspiring and everything was overpriced.

There were two standout places we went to during our trip. The first was [Pão de Canela](https://maps.app.goo.gl/zXLM61CWA4wANP4ZA){:target="_blank"}, a lovely restaurant overlooking Parque Infantil da Praça das Flores. This was a recommendation from a friend who has family in Lisbon and we weren't disappointed. Spending a sunny lunch, overlooking the park with some delicious food was a definite highlight.

The second was [Cerveja Canil](https://maps.app.goo.gl/W2uYcSzuapE2PXhH9){:target="_blank"}, a taproom located in Baixa with a fine selection of beers as well doing some superb food. With a relaxed atmosphere and friendly staff, we enjoyed it here so much, we returned here for a second visit, partly as I managed to leave my cap there!

## Things to do

I mentioned about Lisbon being a walkable city, and I have to say we really did put that to the test during our trip. We spent multiple days wandering around the city, checking out many of the different neighbourhoods. While the sunshine and hills sometimes made this quite hard going, there was always opportunity to stop for the odd drink or ice cream. In addition to exploring near our apartment in Alfama, we also took in Baixa, Chiado, Bairro Alto, Príncipe Real, Avenida da Liberdade and Parque das Nações.

Travelling over to Belém, we explored the sights there, such as [Torre de Belém (Belém Tower)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xFdYiboAL2gXpu4M6){:target="_blank"} and [Padrão dos Descobrimentos (Monument to the Discoveries)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/2RJUkXciHQxpm2RW7){:target="_blank"}, though the area is quite a tourist hotspot, so expect it to be pretty busy at times. While in Belém, we visited the [Museum of Contemporary Art – MAC/CCB](https://maps.app.goo.gl/iUWBTFkEwd3o1QKg7){:target="_blank"} and [Quake - Lisbon Earthquake Museum](https://maps.app.goo.gl/88aTSiiQHQvqWzbo9){:target="_blank"}, both of which we really enjoyed.

On the way to Belém, below the 25th of April Bridge is [LX Factory](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bSQfJKn3nmeAfPKW8){:target="_blank"}, a former industrial textile/printing complex, now a vibrant creative hub. With a mix of independent shops, food and drink, markets and events, there's lots to see and do. So much so, we returned for a second visit. In particular, we enjoyed the [Ler Devagar](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Btp4iPPoE2KFiQRc9){:target="_blank"} bookshop in a unique setting and featuring an old printing press upstairs.

There's a number of outdoor markets in Lisbon and we had opportunity to go the [Feira Da Ladra](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/feira-da-ladra-flea-market){:target="_blank"} and [Feira da Avenida](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/feira-na-avenida-fair-in-the-avenue){:target="_blank"}. Feira Da Ladra was the most interesting, with stalls selling a huge variety of items, both new and old. Feira da Avenida felt definitely aimed at tourists, and while it was quite a pleasant walk along Avenida da Liberdade, the stalls felt very repetitive.

We always enjoy a bike tour when we go somewhere new, and Lisbon was no exception. Prior to our trip, we did some research and found [The Seven Hills of Lisbon by e-Bike](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/experiences/42197){:target="_blank"}, which looked like an interesting tour. Parciularly, as it would explore more than just the riverfront, with the aid of e-bikes.

We also enjoy a boat tour and booked ourselves onto the [Sail the Tagus past Belém Tower with wine](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/experiences/258961){:target="_blank"}, which we timed so we'd be on the river at sunset.

We're not massive beach people, but given the hot weather, we were keen to get out of the city and spend some time at the beach. From Lisbon, there multiple options, depending on how far you want to travel. Initially, we took the train to the beach at [Carcavelos](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qi7Skqonc3VAnj4y9){:target="_blank"}, which we enjoyed, but potentially should have carried onto [Cascais](https://maps.app.goo.gl/L73Ss1PvaBZHKVsq9){:target="_blank"}.

Wanting to explore further, for our second beach trip, we headed across the Tagus to the beach [Costa de Caparica](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JisYMXLLGzUodNd16){:target="_blank"}. I have to say, we decided against a convoluted journey on public transport and took a Bolt taxi there, which did give us chance to cross the 25th of April Bridge. We enjoyed Costa de Caparica much more than Carcavelos, with its large expanses of sand and chilled atmosphere.

We had another trip across the Tagus to Almada, this time taking the ferry from Cais do Sodré and bus up to [Santuário de Cristo Rei (Sanctuary of Christ the King)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/DuN7DqYyjPfygjJz6){:target="_blank"}, the large figure of Christ overlooking Lisbon, with wonderful views across the river.
